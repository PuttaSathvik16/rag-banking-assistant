# Business Case — Retail Banking Product & Policy Advisor

## Problem
A retail bank wants a self-serve assistant that answers customer and front-line staff questions
about account, card, and loan products — fees, features, eligibility, and terms — grounded strictly
in the product disclosures and policy manuals, never guessed. Today, front-line staff and
customers rely on manual document lookup across dozens of PDFs, which is slow and error-prone,
particularly on fee and eligibility questions where an incorrect answer carries compliance risk.

## Target users
- **Retail customers** self-serving via a web/chat channel, asking about their own accounts,
  cards, or loan options.
- **Front-line branch/call-center staff** who need fast, citable answers to reduce handle time
  without needing to memorize every disclosure.

## Corpus description
A synthetic corpus of 33 clause-tagged markdown documents generated by
`scripts/generate_corpus.py`, covering:
- Account product disclosures (8 products)
- Card product disclosures (6 products)
- Loan product terms (6 products)
- Fee & charges schedules (3 documents, split by category)
- Eligibility policy manual (2 documents)
- Customer FAQ (8 documents)

No real customer, account, or card data is used anywhere (Synthetic-Data Rule).

## Domain guardrails
- **Citation-required**: every non-abstained answer must carry at least one clause-level citation.
- **Abstention preferred over hallucination**: when the corpus doesn't support a confident answer
  (e.g., a rate-by-credit-tier question the documents don't break out), the system says so rather
  than guessing.
- **No definitive eligibility/approval decisions**: the assistant describes what policy says, never
  "you will be approved/denied" — that determination belongs to underwriting, not this tool.
- **Out-of-scope handling**: questions outside the corpus (e.g., account-opening actions, real
  transactions) are declined with an explanation rather than answered speculatively.

## Chunking rationale
Token-based windowing (via tiktoken) was chosen to directly optimize for LLM context usage. Clause
bodies in this domain are short, self-contained statements (a fee amount, an eligibility rule)
where accurate token measurement is critical: character or sentence counts are rough proxies that
under- or over-fill the context window depending on vocabulary density and punctuation, whereas
token count directly reflects what the LLM consumes and what the context budget (max_context_tokens)
must constrain. Chunks are sized to 256 tokens with 32-token overlap, ensuring that the final
assembled context respects the 2048-token budget without guessing or trial-and-error. This
strategy keeps retrieval and citation precise at the clause level while maintaining predictable
memory and cost characteristics. See `src/ingestion.py::token_window_chunks` and `config.yaml`
chunking parameters.

## Success metrics
- RAGAS context precision/recall, faithfulness, and answer relevancy over the golden set
  (target: faithfulness and context precision both ≥ 0.75 as a reasonable bar for a domain this
  narrow — tune after your first real eval run).
- Zero fabricated fee/eligibility figures on the golden set's abstention cases (Q19-Q21).
- Every AC-01 through AC-10 traceable to a passing eval-set entry or test.
